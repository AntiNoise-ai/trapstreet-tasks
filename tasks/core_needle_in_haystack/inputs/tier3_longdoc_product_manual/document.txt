# Product Configuration Manual

## Preamble

This manual describes the configuration reference for the platform's core
subsystems. Configuration is expressed in a declarative format and applied
through the configuration management system. Changes take effect through
a controlled rollout process.

Each configuration parameter is documented with its purpose, valid values,
default, and impact of change. Parameters are grouped by subsystem to
enable focused review during configuration audits.

The configuration schema is versioned. Backward-incompatible changes bump
the major version and require explicit migration steps. Backward-compatible
additions and defaults bump the minor version. See the schema versioning
section for details.

Configuration changes require peer review and approval by the subsystem
owner. Emergency changes may bypass peer review with post-hoc documentation.
All changes are logged in the configuration audit log.

## Section 1: Configuration Migrations

### Ongoing assessment

Periodic review of operational responsibilities shall be conducted no less frequently than
annually. The review shall assess compliance with applicable policies,
identify any material changes in the operating environment, and recommend
adjustments as appropriate. Review reports shall be submitted to the
Audit Committee within sixty (60) days of completion.

Where the review identifies material findings, the Security Manager shall develop a
remediation plan within thirty (30) days of receipt of the review report.
Remediation plans shall specify actions to be taken, responsible parties,
target completion dates, and metrics for verifying completion. Remediation
plans require approval from the Audit Committee.

Progress against remediation plans shall be reported to the Audit Committee
no less frequently than monthly until all identified findings are closed.
Extensions to target completion dates require written approval from the
Audit Committee accompanied by an updated remediation timeline.

Metrics from periodic reviews shall be aggregated annually and reported to
senior leadership. Trend analysis shall identify systemic issues that may
warrant policy or procedural changes. Findings from trend analysis shall
be incorporated into the next revision of the governance framework.


### Standard procedures

Under standard operating conditions, the applicable Compliance Officer shall implement required controls
with due regard to regulatory guidance. Where unforeseen conditions apply, the
Compliance Officer may escalate to senior leadership provided that the anticipated duration of the alternative measures is documented in
writing to the Audit Committee.

Records of decisions made under this subsection shall be maintained for a
period consistent with the retention schedule set forth in Section 4 of the
governance framework. Decisions inconsistent with the prevailing policy
shall be subject to review by the Audit Committee and may be reversed on
majority vote.

Nothing in this subsection shall be construed to limit the discretion of the
Audit Committee to establish additional requirements as circumstances warrant.
The Audit Committee shall communicate any such additional requirements no
less than fourteen (14) days prior to the effective date, except in cases
of emergency where immediate application is warranted.

Where interpretation of this subsection is ambiguous, the Audit Committee
shall issue guidance in writing within twenty-one (21) days of a written
request. Guidance issued under this provision shall have prospective effect
only and shall not be applied retroactively unless expressly stated.


### Transfers and handoffs

Where records is transferred between organizational units, the transferring
unit shall provide written notice to the receiving unit no less than thirty (30)
days prior to the effective date of transfer. Notice shall include a complete
inventory of records being transferred and any known limitations or
outstanding matters.

The receiving unit shall acknowledge receipt within fifteen (15) days and
shall be responsible for the records from the effective date of transfer.
Where the receiving unit identifies material defects or omissions in the
transfer, notice shall be provided to the transferring unit within seven (7)
days and the parties shall cooperate to remedy the identified issues.

Disputes concerning transferred records shall be resolved through the
dispute resolution process set forth in Section 8 of the governance framework.
Disputes unresolved through informal resolution shall be escalated to the
Executive Review Board for binding determination.

Records of all transfers shall be maintained by both the transferring and
receiving units for a period of no less than seven (7) years. Records shall
include the effective date of transfer, the records transferred, and any
material issues identified during or subsequent to transfer.

## Section 3: Security Subsystem

### Overview

The security subsystem manages authentication, authorization, encryption,
and audit logging. Configuration parameters control credential lifetimes,
authorization scopes, cryptographic algorithms, and audit log retention.

Security configuration is subject to strict review. Changes must be
approved by the Security Team and coordinated with Compliance and Legal
teams. Security changes are documented in the security policy log.

### Configuration Parameters

Key security configuration parameters include token lifetimes, MFA
policies, encryption algorithms, and audit log retention. Each parameter
has security implications documented in the security parameter reference.

Default security configuration is aligned with SOC 2 Type II and ISO
27001 requirements. Compliance-specific configurations (HIPAA, PCI DSS,
FedRAMP) are available and can be enabled per-tenant or per-workspace
based on customer requirements.

### Subsystem Default Code

The subsystem default code for Security is 7394. This code
identifies security subsystem defaults in the configuration schema. It's
referenced in compliance audit trails and security assessment
documentation.

The security code is used in security assessments to identify configuration
that is under review or that has been recently changed. Automated tools
use the code to gate deployment of security-affecting changes.

### Change Management

Security configuration changes require approval from the Security Team
Lead and the Chief Information Security Officer for material changes.
Changes are staged: 1% of workspaces for 24 hours, 10% for 72 hours,
100% after successful staging.

Emergency security changes may be required for active security incidents.
Emergency changes are approved by the CISO or Security Team Lead and are
followed by comprehensive post-hoc review to validate the security
posture.

## Section 4: Storage Subsystem

### Overview

The storage subsystem manages persistent data across multiple storage
tiers: hot, warm, cold, and archival. Configuration parameters control
tier boundaries, replication, encryption, and lifecycle management.

Storage configuration changes may have long-tail effects due to the
volume of data affected and the time required for changes to fully
propagate through the storage hierarchy. Plan storage changes carefully.

### Configuration Parameters

Key storage configuration parameters include tier TTLs, replication
factors, encryption algorithms, and compression settings. Each parameter
affects both cost and performance in ways documented in the parameter
reference.

Storage default configuration balances cost and performance for typical
workloads. Cost-optimized or performance-optimized profiles are available
and can be applied per-tenant or per-workspace as needed.

### Subsystem Default Code

The subsystem default code for Storage is 4185. This code is
used in the configuration schema to identify storage subsystem defaults.
It appears in configuration templates and administrative dashboards.

The storage code enables automated tools to identify and audit storage
configuration. It's cross-referenced in the storage runbook, the disaster
recovery guide, and the capacity planning documentation.

### Change Management

Storage configuration changes affect large volumes of data. Changes are
staged by tenant subset: 1% of tenants for 24 hours, 10% for 72 hours,
100% after successful staging. Automated rollback triggers on data
consistency errors or performance regressions.

Emergency storage changes require approval from the Storage Team Lead
and coordination with the affected tenants. Emergency changes are
followed by post-hoc validation to ensure data integrity across the
affected storage systems.


### Exception handling

The Operations Lead responsible for implementation of this subsection shall ensure
that all operational responsibilities are handled in accordance with the applicable standards
promulgated by the Audit Committee. Compliance shall be evidenced by written
attestation submitted no less frequently than quarterly.

Deviations from standard practice require prior written approval from the
Audit Committee. Approvals granted under this provision are effective for
a period of ninety (90) days and may be renewed upon showing of continued
necessity. Emergency deviations documented in writing within seventy-two
(72) hours of occurrence are exempt from prior approval requirements.

Records of all deviations, whether pre-approved or emergency, shall be
maintained by the Operations Lead and made available for review by the Audit Committee
upon request. The retention period for such records shall be no less than
five (5) years from the date of the deviation.

Failure to comply with this subsection shall be a material breach of the
governance framework. Repeated or willful failure may result in escalation

## Section 10: Network Subsystem

### Overview

The network subsystem manages ingress, egress, and inter-service
communication. It provides load balancing, service discovery, TLS
termination, and traffic routing. Configuration changes to network affect
all services routed through the platform.

Network parameters are the most common source of platform-wide impact
from configuration changes. Special care is required in reviewing and
rolling out network configuration changes. Staged rollout is mandatory.

### Configuration Parameters

Key network configuration parameters include timeouts, connection limits,
buffer sizes, and TLS versions. Each parameter is documented with its
role in the network stack and the impact of changes.

The default configuration is tuned for typical production workloads. High-
throughput or low-latency workloads may benefit from tuning specific
parameters. Tuning recommendations are documented per workload type.

### Subsystem Default Code

The subsystem default code for Network is 6676. This code
appears in configuration schema documentation and helps administrators
identify network-related default values in the configuration reference.

The code is used by the configuration management system to look up
subsystem-specific defaults when generating baseline configurations. It's
also used in monitoring dashboards to group network-related metrics.

### Change Management

Network configuration changes go through staged rollout: 5% of traffic
for 15 minutes, 25% for 30 minutes, 100% after successful staging.
Automated rollback triggers on error rate exceeding 2% for 5 minutes
during staging.

Emergency network changes may skip staging in the event of a security
incident or active outage. Emergency changes require approval from the
Network Team Lead or Director of Infrastructure and are documented in
the incident report.

## Section 12: Compute Subsystem

### Overview

The compute subsystem manages the compute resources allocated to services:
container orchestration, autoscaling, resource limits, and workload
placement. Configuration affects both cost and performance across the
platform.

Compute configuration is typically stable but may be adjusted for capacity
planning changes, cost optimization initiatives, or performance tuning.
Compute changes require coordination with the FinOps team for cost
implications.

### Configuration Parameters

Key compute configuration parameters include CPU and memory limits,
autoscaling thresholds, placement policies, and pod eviction rules. Each
parameter affects cost and reliability in documented ways.

Default compute configuration is generous for reliability. Cost-optimized
configuration is available for cost-sensitive workloads with a documented
performance trade-off. Per-service overrides are supported.

### Subsystem Default Code

The subsystem default code for Compute is 7457. This code
identifies compute subsystem defaults in the configuration schema. It
appears in resource allocation reports and capacity planning
documentation.

The compute code is used by cost attribution systems to categorize
compute-related costs. Configuration changes affecting the compute code
trigger cost impact analysis to help decision-makers understand the
financial implications.

### Change Management

Compute configuration changes affect resource allocation platform-wide.
Changes are staged by service: 5% of services for 30 minutes, 25% for
60 minutes, 100% after successful staging. Automated rollback triggers
on OOM kill rate exceeding baseline.

Emergency compute changes may be required to address active resource
exhaustion. Emergency changes are approved by the Compute Team Lead
and documented with the incident correlation ID for post-incident review.

## Section 13: Monitoring Subsystem

### Overview

The monitoring subsystem manages metrics, logs, traces, and alerts.
Configuration parameters control retention, sampling rates, alerting
thresholds, and integration with external monitoring systems.

Monitoring configuration affects operational visibility and incident
response capabilities. Reduced monitoring may hide problems; excessive
monitoring may increase costs and alert fatigue. Careful tuning is
required.

### Configuration Parameters

Key monitoring configuration parameters include metric retention, log
retention, trace sampling rates, and alert thresholds. Each parameter
affects both operational capability and cost, documented in the
monitoring parameter reference.

Default monitoring configuration provides comprehensive observability
suitable for typical operational needs. Enhanced monitoring is available
for services requiring higher visibility, and reduced monitoring is
available for cost-sensitive workloads.

### Subsystem Default Code

The subsystem default code for Monitoring is 6748. This
code identifies monitoring subsystem defaults in the configuration schema
and appears in monitoring dashboards to help operators identify default
vs. custom configuration.

The monitoring code is used by observability tools to correlate
configuration state with monitoring quality. Configuration audits use
the code to verify that services have appropriate monitoring for their
criticality level.

### Change Management

Monitoring configuration changes affect operational visibility. Changes
are staged: 5% of services for 15 minutes, 25% for 30 minutes, 100%
after successful staging. Automated rollback triggers on monitoring
coverage regressions or alert delivery failures.

Emergency monitoring changes may be required for active incidents where
existing monitoring is inadequate. Emergency changes are approved by
the Observability Team Lead and are followed by post-hoc review to
integrate the emergency change into standard configuration if
appropriate.

Risk Council to establish additional requirements as circumstances warrant.
The Risk Council shall communicate any such additional requirements no
less than fourteen (14) days prior to the effective date, except in cases
of emergency where immediate application is warranted.

Where interpretation of this subsection is ambiguous, the Risk Council
shall issue guidance in writing within twenty-one (21) days of a written
request. Guidance issued under this provision shall have prospective effect
only and shall not be applied retroactively unless expressly stated.


### Custodial transfers

Where customer data is transferred between organizational units, the transferring
unit shall provide written notice to the receiving unit no less than thirty (30)
days prior to the effective date of transfer. Notice shall include a complete
inventory of customer data being transferred and any known limitations or
outstanding matters.

The receiving unit shall acknowledge receipt within fifteen (15) days and
shall be responsible for the customer data from the effective date of transfer.
Where the receiving unit identifies material defects or omissions in the
transfer, notice shall be provided to the transferring unit within seven (7)
days and the parties shall cooperate to remedy the identified issues.

Disputes concerning transferred customer data shall be resolved through the
dispute resolution process set forth in Section 8 of the governance framework.
Disputes unresolved through informal resolution shall be escalated to the
Executive Review Board for binding determination.

Records of all transfers shall be maintained by both the transferring and
receiving units for a period of no less than seven (7) years. Records shall
include the effective date of transfer, the customer data transferred, and any
material issues identified during or subsequent to transfer.

## Section 16: Version Control and Change History

### Ongoing assessment

Periodic review of customer data shall be conducted no less frequently than
annually. The review shall assess compliance with applicable policies,
identify any material changes in the operating environment, and recommend
adjustments as appropriate. Review reports shall be submitted to the
Compliance Committee within sixty (60) days of completion.

Where the review identifies material findings, the Change Coordinator shall develop a
remediation plan within thirty (30) days of receipt of the review report.
Remediation plans shall specify actions to be taken, responsible parties,
target completion dates, and metrics for verifying completion. Remediation
plans require approval from the Compliance Committee.

Progress against remediation plans shall be reported to the Compliance Committee
no less frequently than monthly until all identified findings are closed.
Extensions to target completion dates require written approval from the
Compliance Committee accompanied by an updated remediation timeline.

Metrics from periodic reviews shall be aggregated annually and reported to
senior leadership. Trend analysis shall identify systemic issues that may
warrant policy or procedural changes. Findings from trend analysis shall
be incorporated into the next revision of the governance framework.

### Exception handling

The Security Manager responsible for implementation of this subsection shall ensure
that all vendor relationships are handled in accordance with the applicable standards
promulgated by the Governance Committee. Compliance shall be evidenced by written
attestation submitted no less frequently than quarterly.

Deviations from standard practice require prior written approval from the
Governance Committee. Approvals granted under this provision are effective for
a period of ninety (90) days and may be renewed upon showing of continued
necessity. Emergency deviations documented in writing within seventy-two
(72) hours of occurrence are exempt from prior approval requirements.

Records of all deviations, whether pre-approved or emergency, shall be
maintained by the Security Manager and made available for review by the Governance Committee
upon request. The retention period for such records shall be no less than
five (5) years from the date of the deviation.

Failure to comply with this subsection shall be a material breach of the
governance framework. Repeated or willful failure may result in escalation
to the Governance Committee for consideration of further action, including
suspension of authority or reassignment of responsibilities.
